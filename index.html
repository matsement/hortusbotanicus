Heritage and future

The Hortus has always been a place where plants, science and society meet.

The garden dates from 1907 and grew out of a long tradition of technical botany. Coffee, sugar, tobacco, rubber and tea shaped the world of plant science, and the Hortus helped make that knowledge visible. More than a century later, the garden still carries that same ambition: to learn from plants and to make new ideas tangible.

The Hortus is part of the Faculty of Architecture and the Built Environment since 1 January 2026, and in 2050 it stands as the showpiece of the Technical University of Sustainability. It is the place where biodiversity research, design, public trust and practical experimentation come together.

Real anchors remain in the historic seed collection, the 1890s archive and the long reading of the city and the Schie. The book Industrie aan de Schie still underlines the cultural and industrial history of the site, while the garden looks forward to de-extinction, living lab greenhouses, AI-managed wilderness plots and more.

Garden zones

  • Living Lab Greenhouse: a controlled test site for climate-resilient crops and pollinator behaviour.
  • De-extinction Garden: a careful discussion space about ethical restoration and ecological memory.
  • Novel Ecosystem Meadow: a field where succession, biodiversity and adaptation are visible to all.
  • Synthetic Biology Window: transparent experiments on algae, microbes and plant-microbe partnerships.
  • AI-Managed Wilderness Plot: sensors and decision systems help visitors read how nature is being cared for.
  • Heritage Garden and Seed Vault: historic collections, seed preservation and stories from the 1890s onward.
  • Coffee-star café: a public meeting point with climate-conscious coffee, tea and local food.

Exhibitions

Exhibitions from all eight TU Delft faculties.

ABE

Nature-inclusive urban futures

Architecture students map how courtyards, façades and neighbourhoods can host biodiversity without losing comfort or urban density.

Connection to the Hortus: built environment research on nature-inclusive gardens and urban ecology.

Try it: sketch a biodiverse façade and compare soil moisture, shade and insects.

Open all year / until March 2051 / Relation: novel ecosystems

CEG

Water, soil and living ground

From flood resilience to living soil systems, the exhibition shows how Delft's delta ecology can be designed around long-term adaptation.

Connection to the Hortus: the garden sits on the Schie and makes the city’s water and soil stories visible.

Try it: test how different planting patterns absorb water and support species movement.

Open all year / until June 2051 / Relation: novel ecosystems

EEMCS

Listening to the forest

AI systems detect sound, movement and temperature patterns to reveal biodiversity in real time and make the invisible measurable.

Connection to the Hortus: smart sensors and machine learning support garden care, pollination and ecological monitoring.

Try it: compare species counts from camera, sound and soil sensor data.

Open all year / until September 2051 / Relation: AI-managed nature

IDE

Bio-inclusive façades

Design research explores plant-supporting facades, fungal materials and climate-adaptive shelter systems for cities.

Connection to the Hortus: bio-inclusive façade work connects design with pollinator habitat, plant health and materials research.

Try it: design a façade pattern and place it in a mini climate chamber.

Open all year / until February 2051 / Relation: synthetic biology

AE

From orbit to habitat

Earth observation, remote sensing and ecological monitoring show how biodiversity can be tracked across large landscapes.

Connection to the Hortus: satellite and drone observations connect air, soil and species movement to local conditions.

Try it: match a real habitat map to a future scenario of migration and climate risk.

Open all year / until April 2051 / Relation: climate tech

AS

Living materials and plant metabolism

Biotechnology and chemical engineering reveal how plant systems, microbial communities and biomaterials can support resilient futures.

Connection to the Hortus: life science laboratories and the garden form a practical bridge between natural processes and forms of cultivation.

Try it: compare nutrient cycles and observe material changes under different growing conditions.

Open all year / until May 2051 / Relation: synthetic biology

ME

Biomimicry in motion

Robotics, soft systems and bio-inspired design show how machines can assist monitoring, maintenance and adaptation in living environments.

Connection to the Hortus: robotics helps with pollination, plant care and low-impact ecosystem maintenance.

Try it: operate a pollination robot and compare its effect on plant movement and biodiversity.

Open all year / until August 2051 / Relation: climate tech

TPM

Ethics, policy and returned species

Governance and policy questions are brought into view: what counts as restoration, how do risks become public decisions and who decides what to bring back?

Connection to the Hortus: de-extinction and novel ecosystems require social trust, public deliberation and policy design.

Try it: vote on a risk scenario and compare trade-offs with biodiversity, cost and public acceptance.

Open all year / until November 2051 / Relation: de-extinction

How to get here

By public transport: a short walk from Delft station and the central tram loop.

By bike: covered parking and direct routes from the city centre and campus.

By car: limited visitor parking near the Schie; use the city ring and walk the last stretch.

Accessibility

The building has step-free access, adapted toilets, hearing support and quiet hours every Tuesday morning.

House rules

Stay on marked paths, respect the living lab spaces and follow staff guidance in wetland and greenhouse areas.

Guided tours and walks

Short tours run daily and longer themed walks are available in spring and summer. Book at the entrance or online.

Coffee-star in the Hortus

Grab coffee, local pastries and seasonal lunches in the public café beside the heritage garden.

School visits and venue rental

School groups, workshops and private events can be booked year-round. Venue rental includes the café, greenhouse and event terrace.
